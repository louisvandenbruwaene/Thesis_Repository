This thesis studied Erd\H{o}s Problem 915 in sixteen variants: simple graphs or multigraphs, edges or hyperedges, undirected or directed, with edge-disjoint or separated routes.
\Cref{tab:conclusion} lists the proved values and bounds together with the constructions that attain them.

For simple graphs with edge-disjoint routes, \Cref{thm:mader} gives $\ell_m(n)\le\floor{m(n-1)/2}$, proved here with a Gomory--Hu tree, and the hub graph of \Cref{const:hub-spokes} attains it for every $n\ge m\ge2$ (\Cref{thm:extremal-existence}).
The same tree gives the multigraph value $L_m(n)=(m-1)(n-1)$, attained by a tree with all multiplicities $m-1$ (\Cref{thm:multigraph-edge}).
A cut argument extends this to $r$-uniform multihypergraphs, with the value $\floor{(m-1)(n-1)/(r-1)}$ for every $n\ge r$ (\Cref{prop:hyper-edge}).
For $r\ge3$ simple hypergraphs reach the same value when $m-1\le\binom{n-2}{r-2}$ (\Cref{thm:simple-hyper-edge}).

For separated routes, the values for hypergraphs and multigraphs are exact at $m=2$ and $m=3$, and there they equal the values for edge-disjoint routes (\Cref{thm:hyper-vertex-m2,thm:hyper-vertex-m3}).
For multigraphs the two kinds of routes give different values at $m=5$ (\Cref{const:multi-bipartite}).
For simple graphs they give the same values for $m\le4$, and for every $m\ge5$ they differ for some $n$. Gluing graphs of S{\o}rensen and Thomassen at one vertex shows that the conjecture of Bollob\'{a}s and Erd\H{o}s fails at $m=5$ (\Cref{thm:bollobas-erdos-false}).

Directed routes make the values grow quadratically in $n$.
At $m=2$ simple directed graphs have exactly $M(n)=\max(2(n-1),\floor{n^2/4})$ arcs, for both kinds of routes (\Cref{thm:dir-arc-m2-exact,thm:dir-vertex-m2-exact}).
Directed multigraphs with arc-disjoint routes have exactly $(m-1)M(n)$ arcs for every $m$ (\Cref{thm:dir-multi-full}).
For simple directed graphs with $m\ge3$, for directed multigraphs with separated routes, and for directed hypergraphs in the forward, backward and general models, the upper bounds hold for every $n$, and the constructions attain their leading term.

The program of \Cref{ch:machine} computed values for $n\le7$ and $2\le m\le7$ in all sixteen problems.
Every value it marks as exact agrees with the results above, and every graph it reports carries a checked certificate.
