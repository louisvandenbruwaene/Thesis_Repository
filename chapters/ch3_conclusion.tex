This thesis studied Erd\H{o}s Problem 915 in sixteen variants: simple graphs or multigraphs, edges or hyperedges, undirected or directed, with edge-disjoint or separated routes.
\Cref{tab:conclusion} collects the proved values.

For simple graphs with edge-disjoint routes, Mader's bound is proved here with a Gomory--Hu tree (\Cref{thm:mader}), and the hub graph attains it for every $n\ge m$ (\Cref{const:hub-spokes,thm:extremal-existence}).
The same tree gives the exact value for multigraphs, attained by a tree with all multiplicities $m-1$ (\Cref{thm:multigraph-edge}), and a cut argument extends it to multihypergraphs (\Cref{prop:hyper-edge}).
For $r\ge3$, a hub joined to a sparse $(r-1)$-uniform hypergraph shows that simple hypergraphs reach the same value (\Cref{thm:simple-hyper-edge}).

For separated routes, the values for hypergraphs and multigraphs are exact at $m=2$ and $m=3$, attained by star hypertrees at $m=2$ and by the constructions for edge-disjoint routes at $m=3$ (\Cref{thm:hyper-vertex-m2,thm:hyper-vertex-m3,cor:multi-vertex-m3}).
For larger $m$ the multitree, the complete and the bipartite multigraph, and the multitree with a triangle give lower bounds for multigraphs (\Cref{prop:multi-three,prop:multi-triangle}), and the bipartite multigraph on four vertices shows that the two kinds of routes differ at $m=5$.
For simple graphs, gluing graphs of S{\o}rensen and Thomassen at one vertex disproves the conjecture of Bollob\'{a}s and Erd\H{o}s at $m=5$ (\Cref{thm:bollobas-erdos-false}).

For directed routes the values grow quadratically in $n$.
The bidirected star and the one-way bipartite directed graph give the exact values at $m=2$ (\Cref{thm:dir-arc-m2-exact,thm:dir-vertex-m2-exact}), and the bidirected tree and the one-way bipartite directed graph with all multiplicities $m-1$ are extremal for directed multigraphs with arc-disjoint routes (\Cref{thm:dir-multi-full}).
The augmented bipartite directed graph (\Cref{const:augmented-bipartite}) and a split of the vertices into tails and heads for directed hypergraphs (\Cref{prop:dir-hyper-first}) attain the leading terms of the upper bounds (\Cref{thm:dir-arc-linear-error,cor:dir-multi-incidence,thm:dir-hyper-constant,thm:dir-hyper-general-constant}).

The program of \Cref{ch:machine} computed values for $n\le7$ and $2\le m\le7$ in all sixteen problems.
Every value it marks as exact agrees with the results above, and every graph it reports carries a checked certificate.
